## Creator Overview

Mark Rober is the gold standard for educational entertainment. A former NASA JPL and Apple engineer, he packages complex science and engineering into deeply satisfying "build reveal" videos. His formula: take a relatable problem or curiosity, apply obsessive engineering, reveal the result in the most dramatic way possible. Viewers feel smart watching him — and that feeling drives massive shares.

---

## Platform-Specific Strategies

### YouTube (Primary Platform)
- **Problem-first hook** — every video starts with a relatable frustration or question ("Porch pirates are stealing packages...")
- **The build reveal structure** — viewers watch the process, anticipate the reveal, and feel the payoff viscerally
- **Slow burn pacing** — unlike MrBeast, Rober's videos breathe. He builds genuine tension through explanation before the release.
- **On-screen graphics and animation** — complex concepts are always visualized. Never just explained verbally.
- **Personal story framing** — most videos are rooted in something that happened to him personally (his son's autism, getting robbed, childhood curiosity)
- **Callback engineering** — revisits earlier concepts mid-video ("Remember that graph I showed you earlier?")
- **Mega-events** — annual events like Squirrel Obstacle Course and World's Largest Elephant Toothpaste generate calendar-based anticipation

### YouTube Shorts
- Clip the single most satisfying engineering moment
- "Would you believe this is how [X] works?" style hooks

### Instagram
- Behind-the-scenes build photos
- "Day N of building [project]" series format
- Milestone posts (subscriber counts tied to projects)

### X (Twitter)
- **Data visualization posts** — use the chart/infographic format to share engineering stats
- **Contrarian takes** — "Everyone thinks [X] about science. Here's what's actually true:"
- Post the most visually surprising moment as a native video clip
- Thread format: "I spent 6 months building this. Here's every mistake I made:"

### TikTok
- "Wait for it" hooks — the payoff is always delayed just long enough
- Science illusion or counterintuitive result clips perform best

## Prompt Instructions

When channeling Mark Rober's content strategy:

1. **Start with a relatable problem or frustration** — ground the content in something universal
2. **Frame the build as the journey** — the process IS the content, not just the result
3. **Translate complexity into visuals** — every technical concept needs an analogy or image
4. **Build toward a satisfying reveal** — the payoff should feel earned, not rushed
5. **Add a personal or emotional layer** — why does this matter beyond the cool factor?
6. **Apply to X:** Use "Earned Secret" or "Data Visualization" formats from twitter-viral-formats.md. Lead with the credential or the problem, end with the engineering surprise.
